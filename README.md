# Market-E-Commerce-Analysis
## Executive Summary:
Stakeholders have requested a review of the total revenue generated by country/discounts and insights into the different customer types so that we can make informed campaign appeals. Using SQL and Power BI, I pulled transaction history data from a SQL server, organized it to show only relevant data regarding revenue, discount values, and countries involved, where the US is by far the largest revenue contributor. Then, using Python with Pandas/Numpy/sklearn, I found that there exist a few distinct clusters of customers with low, medium, and high customer lifetime values (CLV), where those with the highest CLV contribute to more than 70% of the revenue despite occupying a comparitivly small portion of the customer base. 

## Business Probelm:
How to attract customers and which customers to attract are the topical questions. Discounts can garner more sales but offer a revenue trade off, and it may not be worth attracting all types of people to our store. This analysis serves to look into which types of customers should marketing target (from customers already involved in purchases or otherwise), and how should they be appealed to?

## Methodology:
1. Use SQL to extract, clean, and organize the data into a more usable format from the database
2. Produce a dashboard in Power BI that visualizes revenue by country, discount amount, and over time.
3. Load the data into Python to optimize data usage of datasets
4. Use Python analysis tools to calculate common market metrics and observe trends across metrics

## Skills:
SQL: CTEs, Joins, format, custom columns

Power Bi: DAX, ETL, calculated columns, data visualization, data modeling

Python: Pandas, Matplotlib, Numpy, Sklearn, Machine Learning (KMeans Clustering), data visualization, data engineering, memory optimization, feature engineering

## Results:
I found that close to 40% of the total revenue was generated by products under a discount, with a proportionally equivalent distribution across countries. The US is the largest revenue contributor by country. Within the customer data, distinct clusters of customers around CLV and Recency, Frequency, Monetary (RFM) metrics were found, each containing low, medium, and high clusters. From the high clusters most of the revenue is generated, as customers with medium-very high RFM scores generated over 70% of the total revenue whereas the count for these customers was low compared to poor/lost customers. No distinct trend was observed in comparing clusters to age groups, loyalty tiers, or acquisition channel. This frees up allowance to determine which of the methods in each of the categories is most cost efficient to work with, say whichever acquisition channel is cheapest to work with should hold priority in usage. As well, since there was not a significant difference in the average CLV type by loyalty tier, we can focus on getting customers to join the program rather than getting current members to upgrade tiers.
